# NutriSphere Project Vision

## Goal
Build a **professional-grade, product-level** open platform that makes the multi-dimensional relationships between diet, nutrition, biological mechanisms, and health/disease outcomes visually explorable and understandable for ordinary people, while remaining scientifically transparent and evidence-based.

This is **not** a quick demo or academic prototype that will be abandoned. From day one we design for:

- Usability by non-experts
- Scientific integrity (evidence levels, limitations, sources clearly shown)
- Scalability of data and features
- Maintainability and clean architecture
- Clear disclaimers (not a medical device)

## Why This Exists
Most people choose food by habit and taste because the links between diet and health are hard to grasp. Existing tools are either too simple (calorie counters) or too technical (raw epidemiological tables). NutriSphere aims to occupy the useful middle: an interactive 3D space where users can see pathways such as “animal protein / ultra-processed foods → chronic inflammation / oxidative stress → elevated risk of certain chronic conditions” and explore alternatives.

## Quality Bar (Non-Negotiable)

- Professional UI/UX (mobile-friendly, accessible, clear narrative)
- Every visualized claim traces back to a source and evidence type
- Explicit labeling of correlations vs causation, ecological vs individual data
- Modular design so new data sources and mechanisms can be added without rewriting the core
- Documentation and tests from the beginning

## Scope Boundaries
- Educational and research exploration tool
- No diagnostic claims, no treatment protocols, no supplement dosing
- Personalized simulations are clearly marked as models, not clinical advice

## Team & Ownership
Development is coordinated in this repository. Research literature notes and book excerpts live in the dedicated knowledge-base Project for the research assistant workflow. Product code, structured data, schemas, and open-data pipelines live here.
